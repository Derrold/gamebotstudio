How to prompt GameDevelopmentBot

A short guide for GameDevelopmentBot 0.0.28 — your first game, better builds, and working on a project you already made.

Write in plain words, as if you were briefing a teammate. Paste your whole plan in one message: the game idea plus every feature or change you want, as a paragraph or a list. Starting in 0.0.27, GameDevelopmentBot reads all of it, fills in sensible defaults for anything you left out, asks at most one short question (only if it really needs an answer), then builds the game and works through your list in order, telling you what it did for each item.

1. Paste your whole plan in one message

Put everything in your first message: the idea, and then each thing you want in the game. A list works well:

Make a first person shooter called Iron Rain. You're a soldier fighting through a ruined city at night.
- 12 enemies
- a final boss
- make it rain
- add a sniper tower
- a grenade you throw with G that does area damage
- a kill counter on screen
Win by clearing all enemies.

GameDevelopmentBot works out the title, genre, engine, core loop, story, goals, setting and art style from your plan. Anything you did not say gets a sensible default, and it shows you what it picked so you can change it later. It then asks one combined question only when something is truly required:

  • Engine, only if both Unity and Unreal are installed and you did not name one.
  • Assets, only if the game needs something you have no pack for (answer “blender”, “placeholder” or “store”, for all of them or per item).

Anything you don't answer uses the default (Unity, and placeholders for missing packs). Say just do it or use defaults in your plan and it skips the question and starts right away. It never goes back to the start of the questions.

Each item in your list is then done in order, and the reply says what happened to each one: lighting, weather, time of day, colours, enemy counts and bosses go straight into the build; objects you name (a barn, a tower) are modelled in Blender; other gameplay features are written as C# and checked with Unity's compiler first. If an item can't be done, it says so plainly and leaves the rest of the game working. Genre, when you name one, is matched to the official 19: Action, Adventure, RPG, Shooter / FPS, Strategy, Simulation, Sports, Racing, Fighting, Horror, Platformer, Puzzle, Open World, Survival, Sandbox, Stealth, Battle Royale, MOBA and MMORPG.

A short message like “make an fps” still works: then GameDevelopmentBot asks the basic questions one at a time.

2. Good answers vs. vague ones

Specific beats vague. One short, concrete line is more useful than a long but fuzzy one.

Vague

  • “Make a cool game.”
  • “It’s in a forest.”
  • “Fun gameplay.”
  • “Nice graphics.”
  • “You win.”

Better

  • “A third-person adventure called Pinewatch in Unity.”
  • “A small island fishing village at dusk, with a wooden dock and pine woods.”
  • “Search for tracks, sneak close, take a photo, return to the cabin.”
  • “Stylized low-poly, warm sunset lighting.”
  • “Reach the cabin before nightfall with three wildlife photos. You lose if you’re spotted too often.”

For story, a short paragraph is enough; say “light” or “none” if you want almost no story. For art style, one phrase works: realistic, stylized, low-poly, pixel, horror-dark, cute, cartoon, and so on. When you ask for a change later, be just as specific — for example, say which axis to move on, which object, and by how much.

3. The asset step: real models vs. gray blocks

If the scene is full of gray square blocks, those are placeholders. They appear when GameDevelopmentBot does not have a real pack for something, or when you skip the asset step with “just build”, “continue”, or “continue with placeholders”.

On Unity, this step comes before the build. GameDevelopmentBot scans packs you already have on this PC and plans what the game needs (ground and terrain, trees and grass, buildings, props, characters, vehicles, and so on). If a need has no pack, it asks what to do:

  1. Use free Unity Asset Store packs: click Add to My Assets, then in Unity go to Window > Package Manager > My Assets and click Download. Say “open my assets” if you want that window opened for you.
  2. Have it model the item in Blender and import it.
  3. Use a simple placeholder for now.

You can answer for everything at once (“2” or “blender”) or per item (“trees blender”). When the plan looks right, say build to start. Only say “just build” or “continue with placeholders” if you are fine with gray blocks for now and want to swap packs in later.

To get real models:

  • Download the Unity Asset Store or Fab packs you already own before you rely on them. Unity: Window > Package Manager > My Assets > Download. Unreal: Epic Games Launcher > Unreal Engine > Library (Fab) > Add to Project.
  • GameDevelopmentBot scans downloaded packs in the Unity Asset Store cache and packs already inside your own Unity projects under Documents\UnityProjects. It also looks at the Epic VaultCache for Unreal. Packs you own in your account but have not downloaded on this PC will not show up yet.
  • Say my assets to see the list, then a pack number or name to use one. After a new download, say rescan or use my assets — you do not need to restart GameDevelopmentBot.
  • Prefer packs for ground and terrain, buildings and props, characters, and vehicles, plus anything else the plan lists for your genre (trees, weapons, audio, and so on).

GameDevelopmentBot cannot complete a Unity sign-in for you. Sign in to Unity Hub or the editor yourself, then download the packs. One or two downloads is enough to start; it will not bulk-download your whole library.

For Unreal, the pre-build pack plan above is skipped. After the Unreal build, GameDevelopmentBot asks about assets one at a time. You can describe what you want (it can model it in Blender), say “my assets”, say “free” for a CC0 pick (Kenney, Quaternius, Poly Haven), or say “skip”.

4. Turn a picture into a 3D model (new in 0.0.28)

Have a picture of one thing you want in your game — a deer, a car, a watchtower? GameDevelopmentBot can turn a PNG or JPG of one object into a textured 3D model on your own graphics card (NVIDIA or AMD, 8 GB VRAM). Give it the file path in plain words:

Turn this picture into a 3D model for Pinewatch: C:\Users\Me\Pictures\watchtower.png
  • First time: it starts a one-time setup in the background (about 20 GB download; keep about 25 GB free). Keep chatting, and ask again when it says the 3D generator is ready.
  • How long: about 1–2 minutes per model on a fast NVIDIA card, about 6–15 minutes on an 8 GB AMD card (longer if other programs are using the graphics card).
  • Where it goes: GLB and FBX files in the game’s Art\Generated3D folder. On Unity it is also copied into the project under Assets and the game is rebuilt. On Unreal, import the FBX (or GLB) from that folder.
  • Best pictures: one object, front or 3/4 view, filling most of the frame, plain background (the background is cut out for you). Use only pictures you made or have the rights to.
  • No FBX? Install Blender (free) so it can make the FBX; the GLB is always there.
  • Memory: 16 GB RAM works with the Windows pagefile on; 32 GB is recommended. Close big programs while a model generates.

Picture→3D is Powered by Tencent Hunyuan (Tencent Hunyuan 3D 2.0 Community License). GameDevelopmentBot is not available in the EU, UK or South Korea. Don’t use its 3D output to train or improve other AI models.

5. After the build

Keep talking in the same chat, or open that game’s chat from the sidebar. Projects GameDevelopmentBot creates live in Documents\GameProjects. You can paste a list of changes in one message (for example “make it night, add 20 enemies, add a red barn, add a minimap”): it does each one in order on that same game, tells you what happened to each, and rebuilds once — no new questions. Single changes in plain words work too, for example:

  • “Swap the player for the knight model.”
  • “Add trees around the map.”
  • “More enemies.”
  • “The player falls through the floor.”

To open a GameDevelopmentBot-made project in the Unity editor, say open [name] in Unity (for example, “open Pinewatch in Unity”). If something is wrong, describe the bug in the chat; GameDevelopmentBot can inspect the scene, try a fix, and rebuild. After a rebuild, say play when you want to try it. You can also say rebuild if you want a new Windows build, or undo to revert the last brief change (darker lighting, weather, player speed, and similar).

On Unity, after the first build it may also continue with asset questions one at a time (describe it, “my assets”, “free”, or “skip”). You can ask for a change at any time.

6. Work on a game you already built

Starting in 0.0.25, GameDevelopmentBot can work on a Unity or Unreal project you made outside the app. Update to the latest version first.

Click Open existing project in the left sidebar and pick the folder: for Unity, the project folder (the one that contains Assets) or the Assets folder itself; for Unreal, the folder that contains the .uproject file. You can also type a path in chat, for example open my game at D:\Games\MyGame. A path to the .uproject file works too.

It links the project where it already sits. Nothing is copied, moved, or changed by the link itself. A small entry is created under Documents\GameProjects for chat, an overview, backups, and later Windows builds (builds go to that GameProjects folder, not into your project). The linked chat then shows the project’s scenes (or Unreal maps), scripts, and packages.

For Unity, it then asks before adding anything to your project: two small editor-only helper scripts in Assets/Editor/GameDevelopmentBot. They do not change your scenes, settings, or game code, and they are not included in your game build. You can delete that folder any time to remove them. Say yes if you want rebuilds and a detailed scene report from chat; say no if you prefer to add nothing. If you decline, it can still open the project, read scenes and scripts, make changes you ask for (with backups), and build from the scenes in File > Build Settings.

After it is linked, you can say things like:

  • “open in unity” (or “open in unreal”)
  • “what does X do?”
  • “add a day/night cycle”
  • “fix the jump”
  • “rebuild”

Be specific about the change you want — for example, which object, which axis, and by how much. Before GameDevelopmentBot changes a file, it backs that file up under Documents\GameProjects\<Name>\Backups. After a Unity code change, it runs a compile check and will try to fix errors.

You need the project’s exact Unity version installed in Unity Hub (Installs > Install Editor > Archive). Opening the project with a different Unity version upgrades it, and that is not easy to undo. If the matching editor is missing, GameDevelopmentBot will tell you the version to install. You can still ask it to open with another installed version anyway after you have a backup, but the default is to wait for the correct editor.

On Unreal, it can edit C++ only (files under Source and Config). It cannot edit Blueprint assets (.uasset) as text; for those it will explain the Blueprint steps instead of changing the asset for you.

7. First prompts you can copy

A paragraph with your list of wants, then change the details to yours:

Cozy farming sim called Maple Hollow. You plant crops, water them and sell the harvest at the market.
- crops grow a stage every 30 seconds after you water them
- make it sunset
- add a red barn
- a shop stand where pressing E sells your crops for coins
- show a coin counter on screen
Just do it, use the defaults.

A labeled layout still works too (Name, Engine, Genre, Loop, Story, Goals, Setting, Art style), and you can add your list of features under it.

Name: Pinewatch
Engine: Unity
Genre: Adventure
Loop: Search, sneak close, take a photo, and return to the cabin.
Story: The player is a park ranger who explores a forest at dusk and photographs rare wildlife before nightfall.
Goals: Win by getting three wildlife photos back to the cabin before night. Lose if you are spotted too often.
Setting: A small pine-woods park with a wooden cabin, a dirt path, and a lake.
Art style: Stylized low-poly with warm sunset lighting.
Also: add a wooden watchtower, make it foggy, and show a photo counter on screen.

8. Troubleshooting

  • Update first. Picture→3D is new in 0.0.28. Reading a whole pasted plan with at most one question is new in 0.0.27; linking a game you already built is in 0.0.25 and later. Update GameDevelopmentBot, then try again.
  • It keeps asking questions. On 0.0.27 or later, paste the whole plan in one message (a paragraph or a list) in a new chat, or add “just do it”. If you are halfway through the one-at-a-time questions, pasting your plan or saying “just do it” finishes them with defaults and starts the build.
  • Unity or Unreal must be installed if you want GameDevelopmentBot to open and build the project. With Unreal Editor installed, it can create the Unreal project and package a playable Windows build. Blender helps for one-of-a-kind assets. Design and docs still work without an engine.
  • Linked Unity projects need the exact editor version that project was made with, installed in Unity Hub. A different version can upgrade the project in a way that is hard to undo.
  • Unreal Blueprints. GameDevelopmentBot can change C++ in your linked Unreal project. It cannot edit Blueprint assets directly.
  • Free trial: 120 minutes of active use, within 30 days, on one PC. The clock runs only while GameDevelopmentBot is generating, building, or you are using it; idle time does not count. The trial is one game project, Unity only (if you pick Unreal, say “make it in Unity” to keep going). Trial builds play on your PC and show a TRIAL watermark. Installers, desktop shortcuts, Unreal, and Steam or store prep are in the full version. The app needs internet to check in and count your time; if you stay offline too long, the trial pauses until it can check in again. When the time is used up, your project stays on your PC. Buying the $99 license unlocks the same install so you can keep going. No card is required for the trial, and nothing is charged automatically.
  • Start the app and enter your key. After you install, start GameDevelopmentBot and paste the license or trial key from this website (or use the same email you used to start the trial).
  • Owned packs are missing from the list. Owning a pack in your Unity or Fab account is not enough until it is downloaded on this PC. Download it in Package Manager > My Assets (or the Fab library), then say “rescan” or “use my assets”.
  • Gray square blocks in the game. Placeholders were used for missing packs, or the asset step was skipped with “just build” / “continue with placeholders”. Import or download real packs, then ask in chat to use them and rebuild.
  • Internet. You need a connection to install GameDevelopmentBot and Ollama, to download models the first time, to download asset packs, and for the trial to check in. Day-to-day local AI runs on your PC. Optional cloud models in Settings send prompts off the PC and also need internet.
  • Hardware. Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit), and an NVIDIA RTX 20 series or newer, or AMD RX 6000 series or newer, with at least 8 GB VRAM (12 GB recommended). See Requirements.
